Peg Basketa childhood game, online.

An online 3D version of the clothes-peg battle game Nigerian kids play in the compound. Build a peg, send a room link, and battle live on a phone or a laptop. Live since September 2026 with players in 3 countries.

The problem

A game everyone remembers, and nowhere to play it.

Peg battles live in childhood memories. Bringing them online means live turns between two phones on different browsers, with nothing to install and no account to create.

What I built

A 3D workshop and live battles from a link.

Players build pegs in a 3D workshop, then battle turn by turn in rooms that run on Cloudflare Durable Objects. Quick-match, spectating, a daily challenge leaderboard and a public stats page.

Same result on every phone

Chrome and Safari compute Math.sin differently, so throws drifted. Custom math, a hash every turn and a resync on mismatch fixed it.

Live rooms

Share a link and play turn by turn, or quick-match and spectate. WebSocket rooms hibernate when idle, so idle rooms cost nothing.

Measured in public

Players, sessions, battles and countries are counted on a public /stats page.

Architecture

How it fits together

  1. InputMoves and room messages
  2. Deterministic simOwn math functions, hashed every turn
  3. Rooms and dataDurable Objects, D1 database
  4. 3D clientThree.js, Rapier physics, Web Audio

What I learned

  • Floating-point maths is not the same in every browser. Multiplayer games need their own.
  • Keeping the whole game near 1.4 MB matters more than visual polish on mid-range phones.
  • End-to-end tests in Playwright cover the room flow, because multiplayer bugs rarely show up with one player.
